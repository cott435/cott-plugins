## What was tested

That the stop gate reads its section from the implementer's spawn prompt, in the subagent transcript. It diffs that section's paths since the newest review's `Commit:`, reads only that section's marker, and runs the section's unit suite beside its intent suite. It writes `SKIPPED` for a `repo`-scope pytest row, and calls a failure located outside the section's paths `ELSEWHERE`. Its retry text states the 2.2 commit rule. It falls back to 2.1's discovery when the transcript names no section. `--report` is unchanged, and in a real session the gate still refuses a red intent test twice and releases at attempt 3, silently outside a dev-team repo.

## Verdict

Holds, and every row meets its pass bar. In a real session the gate names its section from the transcript, runs the unit suite, states the 2.2 retry rule, and is silent out of scope. The fixture shows that a sibling's file, half-built or dirty, is `ELSEWHERE`, and that a sibling's marker is ignored. `--report` is unchanged. The note's touched-paths decision contradicted two of its own cases. The first resolution counted tracked changes as touched and untracked files as not. On review, the user settled it differently: a failure located outside the section's paths is `ELSEWHERE`, with no touched clause. The gate and the fixtures were changed to that rule, and hook-events was rerun at 96/97, with the same baseline failure, and contracts at 37/37. Row 3.4 does not exercise this rule, so its 8/8 stands (the note's `## Deviations`).
